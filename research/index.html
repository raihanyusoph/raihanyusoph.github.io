Doctoral research

Peace Without Prosperity

Peace Agreements, Development, and Clan Conflict in the Bangsamoro

This project investigates why underdevelopment and insecurity persist despite major peace agreements and decades of assistance. It follows the relationship among agreement design, development programs, rido, and the institutional conditions that shape implementation.

The study combines analysis of major peace agreements and development programs with interviews, focus-group discussions, and descriptive socioeconomic and conflict evidence. Its central concern is the peace-dividend outcome gap: the distance between the institutional promise of peace and the material and security outcomes experienced by communities.

Conceptual contribution

Transdiffusive conflict

Local conflict can travel through relationships, persist during apparent dormancy, and extend liability across families and generations.

The concept of transdiffusive conflict explains why a dispute may remain locally rooted while its effects spread socially, spatially, and over time. It distinguishes the cessation of visible violence from the resolution of the relational system that preserves grievance and risk.
