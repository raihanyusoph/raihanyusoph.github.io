About

History as a way of understanding peace.

I am a practitioner of history and a peace researcher whose work connects the political history of Mindanao with contemporary questions of conflict, development, memory, and governance in the Bangsamoro.

Biography

Raihan A. Yusoph, MIH, LPT, is an Assistant Professor IV in the Department of History at Mindanao State University–Marawi and a PhD candidate in Hiroshima University’s International Peace and Co-existence Program.

A Japanese Government MEXT scholar, he examines why insecurity and underdevelopment can persist after major peace agreements. His research is grounded in community fieldwork and in the historical experience of the Bangsamoro, especially the relationship among institutions, local conflict, development programs, and everyday life.

He earned his BA and MA in History, specialising in Mindanao Studies, from Mindanao State University–Marawi. Before focusing fully on his doctoral research, he served as Peace Education Program Officer at MSU’s Institute for Peace and Development in Mindanao, where he worked at the intersection of scholarship, dialogue, and public engagement.

Approach

Historically grounded, community attentive

My research begins with the proposition that formal institutions tell only part of the story. Agreements, laws, and public programs matter, but their effects are mediated by local authority, kinship, conflict, memory, and unequal access to resources.

I combine historical and documentary research with interviews, focus groups, community-based inquiry, and descriptive evidence. This allows me to connect policy design with how people encounter peace and insecurity in particular places.
